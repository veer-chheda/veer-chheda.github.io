## Abstract

This paper investigates the linguistic and computational relationship between Marathi and Konkani, two sibling
Indo-Aryan languages languages that share lexical, phonetic and
orthographical similarities while differing in resource availability.
Konkani remains a low-resource language which limits the effectiveness of neural machine translation (NMT) systems. To address
this, we employ multiple linguistic similarity measures including orthographic edit distance, phonetic overlap and semantic
embeddings to quantitatively establish Marathi’s proximity to
Konkani. Using Swadesh lists and parallel corpora, we compute
similarity scores and evaluate zero-shot and finetuned translation
performance with state-of-the-art models such as IndicTrans2.
Experimental results demonstrate that Marathi achieves the
highest alignment with Konkani across all metrics; with finetuned
models utilizing Marathi yielded a BLEU-4 score of 32.95 and
a chrF3 score of 72.02. This performance significantly surpasses other Devanagari script Indo-Aryan languages, with Hindi
achieving a BLEU-4 of 26.15, followed by Maithili (24.59) and
Nepali (23.70). Translation evaluations further confirm Marathi’s
role as an effective intermediate language for enhancing Konkani
translation quality. These findings highlight the value of leveraging sibling languages in low-resource machine translation and
provide empirical evidence for transfer learning strategies in the
Indo-Aryan language family.
