## Abstract

Whether language models (LMs) encode personality traits as stable internal representations or merely mimic them through surface-level behavioural shortcuts remains an open question. In this work, we move beyond prompting-based behavioural outputs and adopt a mechanistic interpretability approach to investigate the internal geometry of the Big Five personality traits within LMs. Using trait-conditioned activations from the Big5-Chat dataset, we evaluate layer-wise hidden representations across five open-source model families in both their base and instruction-tuned variants. Through linear probing, entanglement analysis and information-theoretic Minimum Description Length (MDL) compression, we show that personality features emerge early as low-dimensional, distributed semantic structures that compress and separate monotonically with network depth. Importantly, our geometric analysis reveals that several traits occupy overlapping representational subspaces, causing naive activation steering to suffer from severe cross-trait contamination. To decouple these features, we introduce an inference-time Null-Space Projection (NSP) technique that orthogonalizes target trait directions against competing trait subspaces. Triangulated evaluations using both a GPT-5 judge and human annotators demonstrate that NSP-corrected steering reduces trait entanglement. It significantly amplifies intervention efficacy, improving target shift by 25.7% under automated metrics and 35.7% for human raters, while simultaneously reducing unintended cross-trait leakage. Our findings offer a foundation for robust, multi-dimensional behavioural control by bridging the gap between psycholinguistics and representational geometry.
