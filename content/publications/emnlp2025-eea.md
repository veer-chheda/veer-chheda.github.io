## Abstract

Legal documents are characterized by theirlength, intricacy, and dense use of jargon, making efficacious summarisation both paramountand challenging. Existing zero-shot methodologies in small language models struggle tosimplify this jargon and are prone to punts andhallucinations with longer prompts. This paper introduces the Rhetorical Role-based Extract-Explain-Abstract (EEA) Framework, a novelthree-stage methodology for summarisation ofIndian legal documents in low-resource settings. The approach begins by segmenting legal texts using rhetorical roles, such as facts, issues and arguments, through a domain-specificphrase corpus and extraction based on TF-IDF. In the explanation stage, the segmented output is enriched with logical connections to ensure coherence and legal fidelity. The final abstraction phase condenses these interlinked segments into cogent, high-level summaries that preserve critical legal reasoning. Experiments on Indian legal datasets show that the EEA framework typically outperforms in ROUGE, BERTScore, Flesch Reading Ease, Age of Acquisition, SummaC and human evaluations. We also employ InLegalBERTScore as a metric tocapture domain specific semantics of Indian legal documents.
